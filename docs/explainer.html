How shapefit works

Clean polygons from irregular masks · shapefit 0.1.0 · technical explainer

shapefit receives a shape with bad edges. It gives back a clean polygon. A neural network finds the clean shape and its corners. Then geometry changes the network output into a list of corners.

Introduction

Key ideas

The problem

Many shapes must be polygons. Examples are building outlines from aerial photos, rooms in scanned floor plans, and parts in engineering drawings. Software often finds these shapes as a mask: an image where each pixel is 1 (inside) or 0 (outside).

Masks from real data have bumps, jagged edges, gaps, holes and rounded corners. The usual tool, Douglas–Peucker simplification (cv2.approxPolyDP), can only remove points from the outline. It cannot move a point. It cannot tell a bump from a corner. A small distance limit ε keeps the bumps. A large ε also removes real corners.

The shape in figure 1 is from a seed that training did not use. It was selected by a fixed rule. Section 1.6 gives the averages over 700 new shapes.

The idea: learn to remove the damage

Real data does not supply the correct polygon. Thus shapefit makes its own training data (synthetic data). A program makes a random clean polygon, draws it as a mask, and damages the mask. The damaged mask is the input. The clean polygon is the target. The network learns from millions of these pairs.

The process

  1. Framing. Scale and move the input onto a fixed 128 × 128 canvas.
  2. Network. A U-Net makes two images: a clean mask and a corner heatmap.
  3. Vectoriser. Geometry changes the two images into an ordered list of corners.
  4. Output. Move the corners back to the input coordinates.
from shapefit import ShapeFitter

fitter = ShapeFitter("shapefit/weights/shapefit.pt")
polygon = fitter.fit(mask)          # (N, 2) array of (x, y) corners

Part 1

Overview

1.1Training data

The generator makes a new training pair in approximately 1 ms. Thus the network never sees the same pair two times.

  • Clean polygons. Seven types: ngon, rectangle, quad, convex, star, rectilinear and rect_union. Each polygon must have visible corners. See 2.1.
  • Damage. Fifteen damage types, in two stages: geometric damage to the outline, then pixel damage to the mask. A random severity (mean 1) sets the strength. See 2.2.
  • Targets. For each pair: a clean mask and a heatmap with one spot at each corner. See 2.5.

1.2Framing

The input can have any size. shapefit scales and moves each shape onto a 128 × 128 canvas. The longest side of the bounding box becomes 72 % of the canvas width. The centre of the box goes to the centre of the canvas. shapefit keeps the scale and the shift, and uses them at the end to move the polygon back. Training uses the same framing. See 2.3.

1.3The network

The network is a U-Net with 7.8 million weights. The encoder makes the image smaller in four steps (128 → 8 pixels). The decoder makes it larger again. Skip connections copy fine detail from the encoder to the decoder.

The network has two outputs:

  1. The clean mask shows where the true edge is.
  2. The corner heatmap shows where the corners are. A high value means "a corner is here".

1.4From pixels to a polygon

The vectoriser changes the two network outputs into a list of corners. It uses geometry, not learning. It has six steps:

  1. Find the contour of the clean mask.
  2. Find the peaks of the heatmap.
  3. Move each peak onto the contour. Sort the peaks by their position along the contour.
  4. Add corners that the heatmap missed.
  5. Fit one straight line to each edge.
  6. Intersect the lines of neighbouring edges. Each intersection is a corner.

An optional regularisation step can make all corners 90°. See 2.6.

1.5Measuring quality

Five metrics compare the output polygon with the true polygon. IoU measures the area. The other metrics measure the corners. A polygon with too many corners can have a high IoU.

MetricBetterMeaning
IoUhigherArea of overlap divided by the area of the union. 1.0 is a perfect match.
Count exacthigherPercent of shapes with exactly the correct number of corners.
Vertex F1 @ 3 %higherOutput corners and true corners are paired with Hungarian matching. A pair is correct if the distance is 3 % of the shape size or less. F1 combines missed corners and extra corners.
Vertex errorlowerMedian distance between paired corners, as a percent of the shape size.
HausdorfflowerThe largest distance from a point on one outline to the other outline, as a percent of the shape size.

1.8Limits

  • One outer edge only. shapefit does not make polygons with holes. It ignores holes in the input.
  • One shape for each call. For a mask with many shapes, split the mask into connected components. Then call fit for each component.
  • Curves. A circle or an ellipse gets many corners or unclear corners.
  • Resolution. The network sees 128 × 128 pixels. Very small details on a large shape can disappear.
  • Heavy damage. Strong damage can remove all signs of a corner from the mask. The accuracy decreases as the severity increases (table in 2.7).
  • Short steps. On a union of many rectangles, a step shorter than approximately 3 % of the shape size can disappear.
Part 2

Details

2.1Polygon types

TypeShareDescription
ngon12 %Regular polygon with 3 to 8 corners, possibly stretched a little.
rectangle12 %Rotated rectangle. The long side is 1 to 4 times the short side.
quad10 %Trapezoid, parallelogram or kite.
convex14 %The convex hull of random points. 5 to 11 corners.
star14 %Star-shaped polygon with 5 to 12 corners. Some corners point in.
rectilinear16 %Two to four rectangles joined together: L, T, U and + shapes.
rect_union22 %The union of 2 to 6 overlapping rectangles, with 4 to 21 corners.

The generator rejects a polygon if one of these conditions is true:

  • A corner turns by less than 12° (turn angle).
  • An edge is shorter than 6 % of the shape size.
  • A corner is sharper than 15°.
  • The area is less than 8 % of the bounding box.
  • Two edges cross.

Unions of rectangles

Each new rectangle in a rect_union has its centre inside an earlier rectangle. Thus the rectangles always overlap. The result has approximately 10 corners on average.

  • In 60 % of shapes, all rectangles have the same direction. These shapes have only 90° and 270° corners.
  • In 40 % of shapes, each rectangle has its own angle.

The generator also rejects a union that has a hole or that breaks into separate parts.

2.2Damage types

Each damage type has a probability. The severity controls the strength of all types in one pair.

Stage 1: Geometric damage

The generator puts 600 points along the outline at equal distances. Then it moves each point along the normal of the edge.

NameProbabilityEffect
round_corners35 %Changes corners into curves.
jagged75 %Moves each point by a small random distance.
bumps65 %Adds 1 to 5 smooth bumps (out) or dents (in).
wobble40 %Adds slow waves along the edge.
staircase20 %Moves groups of points by the same distance. This makes steps.

Stage 2: Pixel damage

The generator draws the outline as a mask. Then it changes the pixels.

NameProbabilityEffect
bites50 %Removes 1 to 4 round parts from the edge.
gaps30 %Removes a strip along 4 to 15 % of the edge.
blobs40 %Adds 1 to 3 ovals on the edge.
cuts15 %Draws a thin empty line through the shape.
holes25 %Removes 1 to 4 round parts inside the shape.
speckle30 %Changes random pixels near the edge.
specks15 %Adds small dots away from the shape.
pixelate25 %Makes the image coarse. Edges become large steps.
blur30 %Blurs the mask, then makes it binary again.
morph20 %Opens or closes small gaps with morphology.
NOTE

To apply only some damage types, use the only argument. Example: raster_corrupt(mask, rng, 92, only=["bites", "gaps"]).

2.3Framing

  1. Find the bounding box of the shape. Ignore parts that are smaller than 2 % of the largest part.
  2. Scale the shape equally in x and y. The longest side of the box becomes 72 % of the canvas (approximately 92 pixels).
  3. Put the centre of the box at the centre of the canvas.
  4. Keep the scale and the shift. At the end, use them to move the polygon back to the input coordinates.

In training, the bounding box comes from the damaged shape, because only the damaged shape is available in use. Training also adds random changes of ±8 % in scale and ±2 pixels in position.

CAUTION

Use the same framing for training and for use. If you change SIZE or FILL, change them in both. A different framing decreases the accuracy.

2.4The network

  • The encoder halves the image size four times: 128 → 64 → 32 → 16 → 8 pixels. At each step, the number of channels increases, to 512 at 8 × 8.
  • The decoder doubles the size four times, back to 128 pixels.
  • Each skip connection joins an encoder step and the decoder step of the same size.

Input channels

The input has three channels: the mask, the x position of each pixel, and the y position of each pixel. This method is CoordConv. The position channels tell the network where each pixel is on the canvas.

Outputs

The network gives each output as a logit. shapefit changes logits to probabilities from 0 to 1 when necessary.

NOTE

The network does not output a list of corners. A list has a different length for each shape and has no fixed start point. The vectoriser (2.6) makes the list from the two output images.

2.5Targets and losses

Clean mask target

shapefit draws the true polygon at 4 times the resolution, then reduces it. A pixel is inside if 50 % or more of its area is inside the polygon.

CAUTION

Do not draw the target with cv2.fillPoly at normal resolution. That function makes shapes approximately 0.45 pixels too large on each side. On a 128-pixel canvas, this decreases the IoU by approximately 2 %.

Heatmap target

Each corner gets a round spot. The value is 1 at the corner pixel. The value decreases with distance as a Gaussian with σ = 1.5 pixels.

Losses

A loss is one number that measures the error of the network output. Training changes the weights to make the loss smaller. shapefit adds three losses:

LossOutputFunction
Binary cross-entropyclean maskMeasures the error at each pixel. Gives a large penalty for a confident wrong value.
Dice lossclean maskMeasures the overlap of the full shape. Does not depend on the shape size.
Focal lossheatmapMeasures the error at each pixel, with less weight on easy pixels. Gives a smaller penalty to pixels near a corner.

Approximately 6 of the 16,384 canvas pixels are corner pixels. The focal loss prevents a network that outputs "no corner" at all pixels.

2.6The vectoriser

Step 1. Find the contour

Make the clean-mask output 4 times larger. Find its outer edge as a closed chain of points. The larger image gives positions with an accuracy better than one pixel.

Step 2. Find the peaks

A peak is a pixel with a higher value than all 8 neighbours. Keep each peak with a value above 0.25. Calculate the centre of mass of the 3 × 3 pixels around each peak.

Step 3. Put the corners in order

Move each peak to the nearest point on the contour. Remove peaks that are more than 4 pixels from the contour. Each remaining peak has an arclength: its distance along the contour from the start point. Sort the peaks by arclength. The result is the order of the corners around the shape.

Step 4. Add corners that the heatmap missed

For each edge, draw a straight line between its two corners. Measure the largest distance from the contour to that line. If this distance is more than 3 canvas pixels, the farthest contour point becomes a new corner. Repeat until all edges pass. This is one Douglas–Peucker split that starts from the corners of the network.

Step 5. Fit one line to each edge

For each edge, take the contour points between its two corners. Ignore 15 % of the points at each end. Fit a straight line through the remaining points with total least squares.

Step 6. Intersect the lines

Each corner is the intersection of the lines of its two edges. If the two lines are less than 5° from parallel, or if the intersection is more than 4 pixels from the peak, use the peak position.

Optional: regularisation

  • regularize="orthogonal" finds the main direction of the shape. It turns each edge to that direction, or to 90° from it, if the difference is 25° or less.
  • regularize="parallel" finds edges that are 12° or less from parallel. It makes them parallel.
NOTE

With the targets as input in place of the network outputs, the vectoriser gets IoU 0.995 and the correct number of corners on 99.7 % of shapes (IoU 0.993 and 100 % on unions of rectangles). The test is in tests/test_vectorize.py.

2.8The training run

The shipped model trained for 120,000 steps on one NVIDIA RTX 4090 in 74 minutes. Each step uses a batch of 64 new shapes: 7.7 million shapes in total.

≈ 30 msper training step
37 msmean per step, with checks
74 minfor 120,000 steps
7.7 Mtraining shapes

On CUDA, the script uses bf16 mixed precision and torch.compile. Together they make a step 1.7 times faster. 28 CPU workers make the data, at approximately 14 ms per batch.

Every 5,000 steps, the script measures the network on 500 fixed check shapes (seed=10000) and 200 fixed unions of rectangles (seed=10001).

Procedure

  1. Install the package: 
    pip install -e ".[dev]"
  2. Start the training: 
    python -m shapefit.train --steps 120000 --batch 64 --workers 28 \
    --eval-every 5000 --out runs/full
  3. Monitor runs/full/log.jsonl. At each check, the script writes the five metrics for the two check sets. The keys for unions start with union_.
  4. Export the best checkpoint as fp16 (15 MB) into the package: 
    python examples/export_weights.py runs/full/best.pt shapefit/weights/shapefit.pt
  5. fit_polygon(mask) now loads this file automatically.
CAUTION

The training script cannot continue a stopped run. It saves last.pt at each check. After a crash, start the run again.

Option: a fixed dataset on disk

Each shape uses approximately 400 bytes. 200,000 shapes use approximately 80 MB.

python -m shapefit.make_dataset --n 200000 --out data/train
python -m shapefit.train --data data/train --steps 30000 --out runs/shards
Appendix

Glossary

Each term has one meaning in this document.

Arclength
The distance along a curve from its start point.
bf16
A 16-bit number format. A GPU calculates faster with it than with normal 32-bit numbers, with almost the same training result.
Batch
A group of training pairs that the network uses together in one step. Here, 64 pairs.
Binary cross-entropy (BCE)
A loss for yes/no outputs. It gives a large penalty when the network is confident and wrong.
Bounding box
The smallest rectangle, with sides parallel to the image edges, that contains the shape.
Canvas
The fixed 128 × 128 image that the network always uses. See 1.2.
Channel
One layer of values per pixel. A colour photo has 3 channels (red, green, blue). Inside a network, there can be hundreds.
Checkpoint
A file that contains the network weights at one point in training.
Connected component
A group of mask pixels that touch each other. Two shapes that do not touch are two components.
Contour
The outer edge of a mask, as a closed chain of points in order.
Convex hull
The smallest shape without dents that contains all the points. It looks like a rubber band around the points.
CoordConv
A method that adds the x and y position of each pixel as extra input channels.
Corner (vertex)
A point where two edges of a polygon meet.
Decoder
The half of a U-Net that makes the image larger again.
Dice loss
A loss that measures how much two areas overlap. It does not depend on the size of the shape.
Douglas–Peucker
A classic method that simplifies a line. It removes points that are nearer than ε to the line between their neighbours.
Edge
A straight side of a polygon, between two corners.
Encoder
The half of a U-Net that makes the image smaller and finds larger patterns.
ε (epsilon)
The distance limit in Douglas–Peucker. Here it is a percent of the outline length.
F1
A score from 0 to 1 that combines precision (how many found corners are correct) and recall (how many true corners are found).
Focal loss
A loss that gives less weight to easy pixels. It is used when the important pixels are rare.
Gaussian
A smooth, bell-shaped curve. It is highest at the centre and decreases with distance.
Hausdorff distance
The largest distance from a point on one outline to the nearest point on the other outline. It shows the worst error.
Heatmap
An image where each pixel value shows how probable something is at that position. Here: a corner.
Hungarian matching
A method that pairs two sets of points one to one, with the smallest total distance.
Input
The data that goes into the network. Here: the damaged mask on the canvas.
IoU (intersection over union)
The overlap area of two shapes divided by the area that either shape covers. 1.0 means identical.
Logit
A raw network output, from minus infinity to plus infinity. The sigmoid function changes it to a probability from 0 to 1.
Loss
One number that shows how wrong the network output is. Training makes it smaller.
Mask
An image where each pixel is 1 (inside the shape) or 0 (outside the shape).
Morphology
Image operations that grow or shrink a mask by a small shape. "Open" removes small parts. "Close" fills small gaps.
Neural network
A large function with many adjustable weights. Training adjusts the weights so that the function gives the wanted outputs.
Normal
The direction at 90° to an edge, out of the shape or into the shape.
Outline
The boundary of a shape. For a mask, the outline is the contour.
Peak
A heatmap pixel with a higher value than all 8 neighbours.
Pixel
One small square of an image. Here, pixel centres are at whole-number positions (x = column, y = row).
Polygon
A closed shape made of straight edges.
Probability
How frequently something occurs, from 0 % (never) to 100 % (always).
Regularisation
Here: a rule that changes edge directions, for example to make all corners 90°.
Seed
The start number of a random number generator. The same seed always gives the same shapes. A different seed gives different shapes.
Severity
A random value for each training pair that controls how strong the damage is. Its mean is 1.
Skip connection
A path in a U-Net that copies detail from an encoder step to the decoder step of the same size.
Step
One update of the weights, from one batch.
Synthetic data
Data that a program makes, not data from the real world.
Target
The correct answer for one training input.
Total least squares
A line fit that makes the perpendicular distances from the points to the line as small as possible.
Training
The process that adjusts the network weights to make the loss smaller.
TTA (test-time augmentation)
Run the network on rotated and flipped copies of one input, then calculate the mean of the results.
Turn angle
The change of direction at a corner. 0° means a straight line.
Union
The area that is inside at least one of several shapes. Overlapping parts count one time.
U-Net
A network that makes an image smaller, then larger again, with skip connections. Its diagram has a U shape.
Vectoriser
The part of shapefit that changes the network output images into a list of corners.
Weights
The adjustable numbers inside a neural network.
